### Presenter notes

**1 · The spending date.** Imagine this is your household. You have fifty thousand Swiss francs and expect to need fifteen thousand in a year. Before looking at returns, I would ask whether the payment is fixed and in which currency it must settle. We reserve the Swiss francs at the beginning. That single choice changes which portfolios you can actually hold. You can still compare ways of investing the remaining money, and keep additional cash if the investment risk limit requires it. Today we will follow one decision from your answers to a confirmed simulated review.

**2 · Capacity and willingness.** Open the prefilled fictional mandate. The factual inputs produce the liquidity calculation. An earlier bill can require cash sooner even when your attitude toward uncertainty stays the same. The interview asks which one-year hypothetical payments you would choose. You can skip or stop. An incomplete answer leaves the risk interval open. In the full research example, two understood choices narrow the model's coefficient to about 2.67 through 4.44. They refer to a hypothetical stake of a thousand francs. Changing that stake to an entire household requires further evidence. Look at the funding failure as well. Ten thousand francs cannot cover a fifteen-thousand-franc bill even with the entire initial balance in cash.

**3 · The allocation decision.** Both columns refer to the same date, same reserve and same modeled entry costs. In the fixed mix, sixty percent of invested ETF exposure goes to shares and forty percent to Treasuries. The risk limit reduces invested exposure, so the sleeve retains cash. The robust objective discounts estimates whose expected returns are less certain. On this date it gives gold a substantial weight and the bond estimate is weak. Notice how much cash it retains. The reserve is already set aside separately. Fees apply to the traded amount. They are paid out of the same thirty-five thousand francs. The estimated weights change markedly under different samples. Neither a single optimized weight nor a favorable historical average determines what the client should choose.

**4 · The goal.** The final goal is sixty-five thousand francs in initial purchasing power after the separate withdrawal. At two percent inflation the household needs about seventy-six thousand one hundred fifty-eight francs at year eight. The scenarios draw joint blocks of observed ETF and exchange-rate movements and charge costs on transactions. At one hundred francs a month, the robust allocation misses the target on most simulated paths. At two hundred fifty francs a month the median exceeds the target, although about a third of paths still fall short. These frequencies describe this simulation. Ask whether higher saving is realistic, whether the date can move, or whether a lower target would still serve the purpose.

**5 · Execution and evidence.** Suppose the model produces an order at tonight's close. Until it fills, the existing units earn the return and the new units earn nothing. A price gap changes the amount the order can buy. A missed trade also changes future holdings. We compared those outcomes with the same funding and withdrawal. The classical and robust portfolios have positive average retrospective gains versus the fixed mix in the primary case. After allowing for dependence and variation across just seven annual cohorts, the interval includes a loss as well as an improvement. The research models remain available for explanation. The portable monitor accepts a manually selected fictional allocation.

**6 · A review.** Save one example choice and confirm local storage. We can now see exactly which units, cash, model and date define the account. Receive a new close. The next review compares holding with trading from the same marked positions. Change the payment requirement and ask how much cash must be available before securities settle. A proposal lists the intended trade, its cost, the remaining shortfall and the change in estimated risk. You can decline, keep your holdings, or explicitly confirm. When the next FX observation is stale the app retains the last reliable mark. Corrected observations preserve the earlier version. The monitor keeps a shadow comparison so that selecting a relatively good outcome is not presented as proof of trading skill.

**7 · The client's decision.** Imagine the payment becomes twenty-two thousand francs and moves to immediately after entry. In the risk-parity example, the reserve and cash available before any sale settles fall short by three thousand five hundred thirty-one francs and fifty centimes. That is a real difference between a marked ETF value and cash ready to spend. If you had to decide today, which condition could you change first: monthly saving, final target, or the date of the payment? We can rerun each under the same risk limit and leave an uncertain allocation undecided. Take questions about the cost assumption, a delayed settlement and the limits of the simulated histories.
