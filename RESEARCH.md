## Applying the Part 01 decision map

The [methodological map](<Theory/Methodology/Decision_Maps/Partie_01.svg>) guides test selection. The plan already covered adjusted prices, historical availability, time windows, block bootstrapping, multiple testing, and costs. The additions below clarify the test selection process based on the data structure and the decisions to be made in the event of failure.

| Study and decision-map branch | Check to perform and evidence to retain |
|---|---|
| All studies · P1–P3, then F | Count gaps and extreme observations by series and date. Plot distributions and check corporate actions at source before correcting observations. Test point-in-time joins, compounded returns and shared trading sessions. If the availability time is unknown, lag the input and rerun the test. |
| H01/H02 · B/C → V2, V14 | Examine standardized residuals and their squares with Ljung–Box, then test dependence and out-of-sample error. Bootstrap two Sharpe ratios from the same dates with pairing and temporal dependence preserved. |
| H03/H04 · B/C/E → V4–V6 | Test predictor persistence. For two non-nested forecasts use Diebold–Mariano on loss differences with a variance estimate appropriate for overlap. For a nested linear extension under squared loss consider Clark–West. Document assumptions and sample size before interpretation. |
| H05 · A at each date, D/E over time → Q1/Q2 | Count missing characteristics by date and size. Compare complete cases with imputation fitted inside each window. For panel regression, examine dependence by firm and month and use appropriate clustered errors. For portfolio sorts, analyze monthly spreads. |
| H06 · E3 → Q1/Q3 | For abnormal returns around announcements, count simultaneous events, check overlapping windows and adjust inference for cross-sectional dependence. Narrow a conclusion if it depends on one date. |
| H07 · E → V/W | Apply identical prices and fees to competing rules. Add a placebo with compatible dates, vary the execution delay and determine the cost that eliminates the gain. Record failed trials as well as successful ones. |

**Validation sequence for each result.** (1) Check for leakage, point-in-time availability and temporal separation. (2) Measure error or return against the benchmark. (3) Apply an appropriate comparison test, including calibration where relevant. (4) Adjust for the number of trials. (5) Report uncertainty. (6) Test robustness, subperiods and costs. (7) Verify the method on a known case and assess power at the economically meaningful effect size. (8) Archive data, version and decision, then monitor drift. At each step record evidence, limitations and corrective action. Explain any inapplicable branch.

## Common protocol before any trial.

1. Establish a record by formulating a reference article, table, or figure; define the target; specify the expected sense; identify the population; set the horizon; determine the decision date; establish a benchmark model; define the key metric; set the economically useful gain threshold; and specify authorized variations and the abandonment criteria. Make these choices *before* examining the test results.
2. First, take a result from the article or lab regarding its universe when the data allows. Record any differences in period, universe, costs, and definition. Adapting a fixed-income study to IEF/TLT or a long-short strategy to a buying position constitutes an adaptation.
3. Prepareing time-series data with DuckDB/Parquet and pandas or Polars. Preserve observation, publication date, currency, unit, and source. Control dividends, CRSP CIZ exits, historical CCM links, and duplicates. The Compustat export reveals the accounting closing; retrieve publications or test a conservative documented delay. For inflation and activity, search for ALFRED vintages. With a single revised series, limit predictive conclusions. Collect real rates, broad dollar, oil, copper, USD/CHF according to the open study. A spot price represents an indicator; a position requires a vehicle and its investable yield.
4. Select dates after reviewing availability. Separate development, internal validation, and final testing for dedicated use. Eliminate overlapping targets at boundaries. Estimate normalization, selection, PCA, and hyperparameters solely based on historical data. In a panel, separate entire months. Mark exploratory relationships that have already been examined between 2005 and 2025, including VIX-ETF. For a test that has already been reviewed, wait for new observations or use a justified independent validation.
5. Deciding at the end of the month with the available information; model the subsequent performance and apply weights only to returns following that performance. Allow weights to evolve between transactions. Use a common window for each “duel” and publish the extended histories separately. Record only the crises that were actually covered after the initial warm-up.
6. Archive raw and clean results, including an effective interval, a specified uncertainty range, pre-defined sub-periods, and the total number of variants. Use block bootstrapping for paired differences; preserve the cross-sectional data for a single month. Control multiple comparisons within families (e.g., using the Holm method). Test reproducibility under constant configuration.

### ETF benchmark schedule

Version 1 uses SPY, IEF and GLD in USD. Data from 2005 onward supply the initial estimation windows. The accounting pilot spans January 2010 to December 2015. At each month’s last close, the long-only global minimum-variance (GMV) portfolio estimates sample covariance from the previous 756 sessions. The other targets are 60% SPY and 40% IEF, and one third in each ETF. Execute all three at the next close. Each simulation starts with USD 100 in cash. Apply fees of 0, 10 and 25 bp per dollar actually bought or sold, including entry. Terminal wealth values the positions held at the closing price.

| Period | Use |
|---|---|
| January 2010–December 2015 | Check calculations, fees and timing. Compare the benchmarks over six complete years. |
| January 2016–October 2026 | Subsequent historical validation using successive windows. Treat this as retrospective because several market–macro relationships have already been examined in these data. |
| December 2026–November 2027 | Prospective window set on November 21, 2026 for all three ETF benchmarks. Record the first target after November’s final close and before the first December closing execution. Save each target and data version before observing the return. |

At the end of the window, report terminal wealth at 10 bp, realized volatility, drawdown, turnover and sensitivity at 0 and 25 bp for each benchmark. Twelve months provide a descriptive prospective check. Label any replay of a decision without a dated log entry as a reconstruction. Freeze each H01–H07 variant before observing its first evaluation outcome. StockTwits coverage ends in March 2020.

## Comparison protocol for H01–H07

Before validation, record the dates, window, assets, primary model and minimum economically meaningful margin δ. Define a paired gain for each period as benchmark loss minus candidate loss, or candidate benefit minus benchmark benefit. Positive values favor the candidate. Report the estimate and a dependence-adjusted interval. Prespecify the promotion rule, test family and treatment of missing data. A lower bound above δ supports a material gain. An interval crossing δ leaves the conclusion open. Equivalence requires prespecified margins and an appropriate test.

**Risk measurement convention.** For h sessions and reference weights w, use the sum of squared daily portfolio returns as the integrated-variance proxy when daily expected returns are negligible. For the centered version, calculate innovations using only past information. Forecast the same quantity by summing daily conditional variances as viewed from the forecast origin. Include relevant autocovariances when estimating the variance of a cumulative return. For GARCH, specify origin/target alignment, distinguish innovation variance from process variance and verify support for multi-step forecasts. Simulate if needed. Divide variance fitted on percentage returns by 10,000 to convert to decimal-return variance. [Forecasting documentation](https://arch.readthedocs.io/en/latest/univariate/forecasting.html)

## Planned experiments

### H01 — Does covariance shrinkage improve allocations?

- **Sources and benchmark.** Replicate [Ledoit and Wolf’s shrinkage approach](https://www.ledoit.net/Honey_2004.pdf) and include the [DeMiguel, Garlappi and Uppal 1/N benchmark](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhm075). Use the same ETF universe, constraints, return inputs and rebalancing dates for each estimator.
- **Calculation.** Compare rolling sample covariance, shrinkage and EWMA. Record the target. The paper uses constant correlation, while some implementations use a scaled identity. Test GMV, then ERC, changing one component at a time.
- **Decision.** Compare conditioning, future risk forecast error, turnover, realized risk and net utility at equal budgets. Retain an estimator if it consistently improves adherence to the risk target. Otherwise retain the benchmark.

### H02 — Does forecasting risk improve exposure decisions?

- **Source and benchmark.** [Moreira and Muir](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22208) adjust exposure using recent variance. Compare fixed exposure with exposure inversely proportional to lagged variance in the same sleeve. Document calibration, financing and leverage cap.
- **Models.** Start with rolling volatility and EWMA, then use GARCH(1,1)-t and GJR with `arch`. Test DCC across equities, bonds and gold as a multivariate variant. Backtest risk forecasts before allocations. Check covariance matrices for positivity and stability.
- **Metrics.** Use QLIKE for monthly variance proxied by summed squared daily returns. Measure volatility error, risk-band breaches, costs and net utility. A more accurate forecast may be useful for monitoring even if reallocation costs outweigh the benefit.

### H03 — Do macroeconomic conditions help explain bonds and cross-asset dependence?

- **Source and adaptation.** [Ludvigson and Ng](https://www.nber.org/papers/w11703) study macro factors and excess bond returns. Reproduce their horizon and variables if available. Document the monthly IEF/TLT adaptation separately. Compare the historical mean, yield curve alone and yield curve with macro factors.
- **Method.** Fit PCA to interest rates, inflation, activity, dollar and credit variables in the past window, then fit a predictive regression. For exposures of all portfolios, use contemporaneous regressions with HAC errors and stress-period correlations. Test real rates and the dollar for GLD, and oil and copper for energy and industrials. Both extensions are exploratory. Use a small VAR and local projections at 1/3/6/12 months to study innovation propagation. Test stationarity and residuals and vary identification order. Use VECM only for an economically justified, cointegrated relationship in levels.
- **Decision.** Report coefficients and uncertainty, then out-of-sample R² for future outcomes. A robust contemporaneous relationship supports risk attribution. Tactical use requires both predictive and economic gains. Compare an observable stress rule with a filtered HMM before changing weights.

### H04 — Do valuation ratios and macro variables forecast returns better than the historical mean?

- **Source.** Replicate the recursive comparison of [Goyal and Welch](https://www.nber.org/papers/w10483). Start with the monthly equity premium. Benchmark against the expanding mean and test one lagged predictor at a time, starting with a rate or spread. Use a valuation ratio only with historically available inputs.
- Variations and testing. Linear regression followed by ridge regression with `statsmodels` and `scikit-learn`. Check for the persistence of predictors and sensitivity to periods. R² outside the sample, paired errors, and net allocation gain. For 80% intervals, measure width, coverage, and quantile score during crises and overall.
- **Decision.** If the historical mean remains competitive, keep it for allocation and report the uncertainty. A retained signal must add value beyond changing the portfolio’s market exposure.

### H05 — Does WRDS stock selection add value after costs?

**Research input.** The H05 cell builds the security-month view `h05_panel`. Mask at, be, gp, ag and bm when the assumed accounting availability date falls on or after the decision session. Keep these securities in the coverage denominator. Recompute each signal’s eligibility after masking. Retain the original investability flag for attrition auditing. A later point-in-time join could retrieve the most recent figures actually published beforehand. Assumed lags and possible restatements limit validation.


- **Source.** [Gu, Kelly and Xiu](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25398) compare panel prediction methods. RiskMatch uses a narrower universe and feature set. Start by reproducing sorts from the course bibliography on book-to-market, gross profitability, asset growth and 12–1 momentum.
- **Data and variants.** Use DuckDB for CRSP/Compustat/CCM. Prespecify eligibility, NYSE breakpoint rules, value weighting, sector exposure and the microcap filter. Compare a univariate sort, a linear combination, elastic net and shallow boosting. Test macro interactions separately. Use the high-minus-low spread as a diagnostic and simulate the long-only sleeve for the client mandate.
- **Metrics.** Monthly rank correlation, quantile monotonicity, factor betas and alpha with uncertainty, turnover, concentration and net return. Substitute the sleeve into an otherwise identical allocation at the same equity weight. Reject gains confined to microcaps, a single period or gross returns.

### H06 — Does a view derived from LN7 add information?

- **Replication.** Reproduce a lab result using the market model, abnormal returns and an event study. Record conventions and deviations from the lab result. Test ETF tags separately from the company-to-sector signal, which requires verified historical links.
- **Adaptation.** Estimate relative views and view uncertainty on training/validation data. Compare Black–Litterman with and without views using the same reference portfolio and tracking-error budget. Control for market and momentum exposures, coverage and costs.
- **Decision.** Outside StockTwits coverage, which ends in March 2020, revert to the no-view prior. Keep fragile results in the lab. Classify FOMC texts only after registering a separate hypothesis and measure of success.

### H07 — Do rebalancing bands preserve gains after costs?

- Adapt the methods for reducing exchange [Novy-Marx and Velikov](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhv063). For a fixed signal, compare monthly, quarterly, and a calibrated zone of inactivity based on validation.
- **Trade accounting.** Measure purchases and sales from drifted weights. Proportional cost equals the sum of absolute trade amounts times the unit cost. Test 0/10/25 bp, then observable half-spreads plus commissions and sensitivity to market impact. Selling 10% and buying 10% at 10 bp costs 2 bp of capital. Account for FX and financing separately. Document excluded taxes and avoid deducting an ETF’s internal fees twice.
- Decision. Compare cost gain, risk gap, concentration, and comparable net risk utility. Verify that a band does not prevent protecting an essential withdrawal.

## From research to a client decision

1. **Forecasts (A).** Export means, variances, quantiles and subsequent outcomes by forecast date, model and horizon. Measure historical exposure, prospective accuracy and calibration separately. Bootstrap residuals jointly across assets to preserve dependence in simulated paths.
2. **Allocations (B).** Test 60/40 (specify SPY/IEF), 1/N and GMV first. Change one element at a time: covariance estimator, macro forecast, WRDS stock selection or turnover rule. Compare ERC, constrained mean–variance, Black–Litterman with and without views, and scenario-based Expected Shortfall optimization. Compare hedging strategies with a simple reduction in risky exposure.
3. **Net frontier.** Sweep volatility caps with CVXPY. At each cap maximize expected return after anticipated costs of trading from current holdings, over the same horizon. Check the weights sum, liquidity, exclusions, concentration, leverage and infeasible cases. Verify monotonicity of the optimal value. Reestimate the frontier on resampled data to show its uncertainty. Show other feasible allocations alongside it.
4. **Mandate (C).** Estimate a range of φ from understood financial choices. Maximize U = μ_net − φσ²/2 on the admissible frontier for each plausible value. Check σ* = Sharpe/φ only in the theoretical Tobin case with a risk-free asset, then apply actual constraints. Separate the desired return, return required by the cash flows and forecast distribution. Simulate withdrawals in the goal currency. Measure shortfalls, risk-band breaches, duration and severity. Show conflicts and nearby alternatives with costs. Check 80% coverage as well as mean outcomes.
5. **Monitoring.** Freeze the decision record on the selection date. Update value, cash flows, net performance, benchmark, drawdown and risk at the latest available close. Timestamp observations. Explain each alert and compare costs and risks before and after a proposed rebalance. Confirm the change with the client. Intraday monitoring requires an authorized, timestamped feed.

Intervals based on six- and twelve-month blocks include zero. Shrinkage improves the median condition number from 12.93 to 9.94. Retain both estimators for research.

At the December 31, 2015 decision, jointly resample the 756 most recent daily return vectors using circular blocks of 10, 20 and 40 sessions. All three illustrative aversion values and all three block lengths use the same long-only mandate, initial holdings and cost of 10 bp. Measure per-asset percentiles and utility lost by the initial target relative to each draw’s optimum. These marginal bounds describe parameter sensitivity on that date. They neither specify a jointly feasible allocation nor give a future return interval.

Two variants minimize trading within the utility tolerance. One caps risk at the optimum’s risk. The other allows any solution under the mandate cap. Compare both with holding and fully moving to the optimum from the same admissible initial positions. Review monthly, measure utility over one year and charge fees on the amount traded.
